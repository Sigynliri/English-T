💬 1. Before You Listen

💡 Tips

Tip 1

Take large pieces of peel and place them along window sills and cracks outside your house, to stop spiders, ants, and other unwelcome guests from coming in.

Tip 2

Cats also really dislike the strong smell of lemons, so you can use lemon peel or lemon juice to keep them away from specific areas in your house or garden.

Tip 3

Newspaper can absorb all sorts of moisture and smells. Just crumple a piece of newspaper and put it inside your food container, then seal the container and leave it overnight.

Tip 4

You can also use the same method to deal with smelly trainers. Just stuff them with newspaper overnight and they’ll be smell-free the next day.

Tip 5

You could buy fabric conditioner to help to restore that softness, but you can use a tennis ball. Just put the ball in the dryer with your towels or sheets.

Tip 6

Try beating an egg with a bit of olive oil, and apply it to your hair. Leave on for about 20 minutes and then rinse with warm water.

Tip 7

But you might be surprised to learn that it is also a great alternative to shaving cream. Just rub it on your skin and shave normally.

Форма Использование Пример
Can Ability, possibility, and permission.
Modal verb. Only has a present form (which can be used to talk about the future) and a past / conditional form (could).

Can you / I...? — to make requests or ask for permission. 		I can swim. (ability)
I can help you tomorrow. (possibility / future)
Can I borrow your pen? (permission)
Can you open the window? (request)
can't To say we are sure that something is impossible / not true (deduction).
The opposite of can't in this sense is must. 		It can't be broken! I only bought it last week. (I'm sure it's not true)
They can't be back yet. They said they were coming home on Sunday. (I'm sure it's impossible)
couldn't To talk about ability on one specific occasion in the past.
We use couldn't for negative past ability (but not could for positive specific occasions). 		I couldn't finish the test. (negative ability on a specific past occasion)
be able to 1. For all other tenses and forms (where can cannot be used).
2. In the present and past if we want to be more formal. 		I will be able to help you tomorrow. (future — all other tenses)
I have been able to finish the task. (present perfect — all other tenses)
I am able to attend the meeting. (formal present)
She was able to complete the report. (formal past)
